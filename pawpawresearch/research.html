Our Research Projects

The Pawpaw Research Alliance coordinates and supports studies that address key knowledge gaps for Asimina triloba. Below are the primary active and recent initiatives. Findings are shared through technical reports, grower workshops, and peer-reviewed publications whenever possible.

Current Priority Projects

  1. Genetic Diversity Mapping — Collecting leaf and fruit samples from wild populations across the native range to identify unique genotypes and regions of high diversity. Results guide seed banking and breeding decisions.
  2. Pollination Ecology — Documenting effective pollinators (primarily small beetles) and testing methods to improve fruit set in both wild stands and cultivated orchards. Hand-pollination trials and attractant studies continue.
  3. Cultivar Evaluation Trials — Multi-year plantings comparing named cultivars and promising wild selections for yield, flavor, disease resistance, and cold hardiness under different soil and light conditions.
  4. Post-Harvest Handling — Investigating storage temperatures, packaging, and ripening protocols to extend the short shelf life of fresh pawpaw fruit for local markets.
  5. Climate Resilience Assessment — Modeling how shifting temperature and precipitation patterns may affect the northern and southern edges of the current range and identifying adaptive traits.

Past Highlights

Earlier Alliance work contributed baseline data on clonal reproduction via root suckers, documented regional differences in fruit chemistry, and helped establish best practices for transplanting container-grown seedlings. Several of these findings now appear in extension publications used by home gardeners and small-scale growers.

Researchers interested in collaboration or access to Alliance data sets may contact us through the Contact page. We welcome proposals that advance conservation or practical cultivation of the North American pawpaw.
